Plenty of food. Too many empty plates.

India wastes food at weddings, hotels and homes every day, while millions go to bed hungry. The food exists. What’s missing is a fast link between the kitchen with extra and the kitchen that needs it.

~78 million tonnesof food wasted in India each year (UNEP Food Waste Index 2024, approx.)
~190 millionpeople undernourished in India (FAO SOFI, approx.)

Figures are rounded from UN reports; check the latest editions before publishing.

One bridge between surplus and need.

Donors list what they have. Verified NGOs claim it. A volunteer or the donor’s own driver carries it across. Everyone sees the status until the food is served.

Freshness checklist: what to tell us

Complete details help NGOs decide quickly and safely. Always follow your local food safety rules.

🕒Food preparation time

Say when the food was cooked or prepared, with the time and not just the date. NGOs use it to judge how long the food can safely wait.

📦Packing time

Note when the food went into containers. Use clean, covered containers and let steam clear before sealing so food doesn’t spoil faster.

🌰Ingredients and allergens

List the main ingredients and flag common allergens such as nuts, dairy, gluten, egg and soy. Mention if the food is vegetarian or contains meat.

🧊Storage conditions

Tell us how the food has been kept (hot, chilled or room temperature) and for how long, so the NGO knows what to expect on arrival.

⏰Pickup deadline

Choose the latest time the food can be collected and still be fresh. FoodBridge works backwards from this deadline to find an NGO and a volunteer in time.
